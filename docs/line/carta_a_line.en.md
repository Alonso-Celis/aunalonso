## To Line Marsa: a letter born of the error.

### 1. Introduction.

There we were, sitting, in the east of Paris, on a terrace neither of us knew. From the moment I left the apartment a mixture of excitement and nervousness was becoming visible in my body: I did not stop perspiring on the way and the temperature was not that high. I fear I was perspiring so much because of the excitement and the uncertainty of the encounter.

### 2. A new notion: too much.

The conversation quickly turned into an exploration of your years before Paris, the intersection between the German world and the world of the former USSR your parents come from. The further you went into your account, into your encounter with the Chanson Française, into your early leaning towards singing but above all towards exploring the world in an unusual way, the more you did it the intensity of your words produced a kind of separate universe, of a landscape I wanted to enter and did not want to leave.

Every answer to my questions turned into uninhabited atmospheres, at moments indecipherable, of unknown languages. Somehow I was beginning to know you through this universe you were telling. It was barely the beginning and I already felt I was wandering in those imaginary rooms, in those hours stuck in a past of yours and yours alone; in every detail of what you said I found the confirmation of a world I had only thought from literature. There you were, drawing your world in every sentence. And it is then that a dimension began to assert itself. Too much, yes, you are too much. We repeated it several times, even with humour. Too much. And I want more.
